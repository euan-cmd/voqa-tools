Range added by charger and dwell time

AC charging is calculated up to 100% SoC, DC up to 80% SoC (SoC = state of charge, i.e. how full the EV battery is). Because DC stops at 80%, longer stays on any rapid charger end at the same figure; a faster charger just gets there sooner. Miles are real-world estimates, so they will be lower than the car's official WLTP range.

Dwell Time
7.4kW: Ideal for overnight parking, employee charging, or locations with longer dwell times. Lower installation costs.
11kW: Good balance for 2-4 hour stays like shopping centres, restaurants, or workplace charging. Most EVs can use this power level.
22kW: Best for 1-2 hour visits, providing meaningful range for shorter stops. Important: Many EVs can only charge at 11kW maximum on AC chargers, so the actual charging speed may be limited by the vehicle, not the charger.
50kW: Rapid DC charging for quick stops (30-60 minutes). Requires significant electrical infrastructure investment.
150kW and 200kW: Ultra-rapid DC charging for short stops (10-30 minutes), such as motorway services and forecourts. Many cars can't take the full 200kW, so the car often sets the speed, not the charger. Needs a high-capacity grid connection.
AC Charging Limitation: Most EVs have onboard chargers that limit AC charging to 11kW, regardless of the charger's capacity. Always verify your vehicle's AC charging specifications before expecting 22kW charging speeds.
